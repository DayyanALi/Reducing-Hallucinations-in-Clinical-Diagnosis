## Subjective

**Chief Complaint:**  
Blinding headache for three days.

**History of Present Illness:**  
The patient reports a sudden onset of a severe, blinding headache that started three days ago upon waking. The headache is persistent, described as mostly an 8/10 in severity, sometimes decreasing to 5/10, and is localized all over the head but stronger over the right eye. The headache does not completely resolve with ibuprofen, which provides only slight relief. Associated symptoms include photophobia (light sensitivity) and intermittent visual disturbances described as shooting lights or "weird" vision. There is no nausea or vomiting, no tingling, numbness, weakness, or changes in smell or taste. The headache is present constantly but does not wake the patient at night, although it is present upon awakening. Occasionally, standing up quickly produces a stabbing pain and a sensation of something moving inside the head. The patient denies recent cold symptoms, sore throat, ear pain, sinus pain, cough, phlegm, nosebleeds, or hemoptysis. 

**Review of Systems (positives and negatives):**  
- Positive: Photophobia, visual disturbances, stabbing pain with positional change.  
- Negative: No nausea, vomiting, neurological deficits, fever, sore throat, cough, or weight changes.

## Assessment

1. **Persistent severe headache with photophobia and visual disturbances, possible migraine:**  
The patient presents with a new severe headache ongoing for three days, associated with photophobia and unusual visual symptoms. History and examination pending but migraine appears likely given family history and symptomatology. Other causes not documented; urgent neurological assessment warranted to exclude secondary causes.

2. **Use of oral contraceptive pill with headache:**  
No increased headache frequency reported since starting pill 1 year ago. However, pill-related risks to be evaluated at further visit.

## Plan

**Problem 1: Persistent severe headache, probable migraine**  
- Schedule clinical appointment for detailed neurological examination including fundoscopy.  
- Prescribe a stronger analgesic than ibuprofen (medication name and dose not documented) and paracetamol to be taken with food for symptom relief.  
- Patient advised to monitor headache severity and pattern.  
- Educate to seek emergency care if headache worsens rapidly, neurological symptoms develop, or if headache becomes unbearable.

**Problem 2: Oral contraceptive use**  
- Blood pressure to be checked at next appointment to assess pill suitability.  
- Counsel regarding potential contraindications of the pill in migraine with aura (flashing lights suggestive).  
- Continue current pill until reassessment.

**Follow-Up:**  
- Patient to attend booked appointment for full assessment.  
- Patient may cancel appointment if symptoms resolve.  
- Contact clinic or emergency department if condition deteriorates before appointment.

**Patient Instructions:**  
- Take prescribed medications with food.  
- Monitor symptoms and return if worsening or new symptoms appear.  
- Maintain hydration and rest as able.
