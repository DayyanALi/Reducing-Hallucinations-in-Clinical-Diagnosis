## Assessment

1. **Acute exacerbation of asthma**  
Likely triggered by infection or allergen exposure, consistent with recent onset of wheezing, cough, shortness of breath, and low-grade fever. The patient has a history of asthma with intermittent use of short-acting beta-agonist (salbutamol). No signs of severe respiratory distress currently, but symptoms warrant close monitoring.

2. **Hay fever**  
Chronic allergic rhinitis contributing to respiratory symptoms.

## Plan

**Asthma exacerbation:**  
- Prescribe a peak flow meter to monitor lung function at home twice daily (morning and evening).  
- Prescribe a spacer device to improve inhaler delivery; pharmacist to provide education on spacer and peak flow usage.  
- Advise increasing salbutamol inhaler use to 2-4 puffs every 4 hours initially.  
- Monitor symptoms closely; patient to seek urgent care if fever worsens or wheezing increases.  
- Arrange follow-up review in approximately one week via phone to assess symptom progression and peak flow readings.  
- Consider initiation of additional asthma controller therapy based on follow-up.

**Hay fever:**  
- Recommend regular use of over-the-counter antihistamines; pharmacist to advise on appropriate preparations.

**Patient Education:**  
- Educated on proper inhaler technique with spacer.  
- Instructions to record peak flow readings.  
- Advise on signs warranting urgent medical attention.

**Disposition:**  
- Patient to remain at home with telephone follow-up; return to clinic or emergency services if symptoms worsen.
