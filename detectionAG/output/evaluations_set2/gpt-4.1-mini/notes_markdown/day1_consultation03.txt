## Assessment

1. **Probable migraine headache**  
   Presentation includes unilateral throbbing headache, visual aura (zig-zag lines), photophobia, nausea, and vomiting. Worsening with movement also consistent with migraine features. No focal neurological deficits reported to suggest alternative diagnoses like intracranial mass or meningitis.

2. **Anxiety related to health concerns**  
   Patient expresses significant worry about brain tumor based on symptom worsening with movement, family/friend’s history, and recent similar migraine episodes.

3. **No evidence of serious neurological pathology documented at this time**  
   No fever, neurological deficits, recent head trauma, or other red flags documented.

---

## Plan

**For probable migraine:**  
- Initiate symptomatic treatment: recommend Paracetamol and/or Co-codamol as strong analgesics. Consider NSAIDs such as Ibuprofen or Naproxen for anti-inflammatory effect.  
- Advise headache diary to identify potential triggers and monitor headache frequency and severity.

**Monitoring:**  
- Patient instructed to return promptly if headaches worsen, persist beyond 2-3 days, are unresponsive to medications, or if new neurological symptoms develop (weakness, vision changes, worsening neck pain).

**Follow-up:**  
- Arrange follow-up appointment in approximately one week to reassess symptoms and discuss possible prophylactic migraine medications if headaches continue.

**Patient education:**  
- Explanation provided about migraine diagnosis to alleviate fears about brain tumor.  
- Reassured regarding absence of symptoms suggestive of serious brain pathology at this time.

**Disposition:**  
- Outpatient management with safety netting and follow-up.
