# Assessment

- **Headache, probable migraine:** Patient’s 3-day history of worsening headache localized mostly over the right eye, associated photophobia and nausea, family history of migraine, and partial historical response to NSAIDs supports this diagnosis. Differential includes tension headache and secondary causes but no red flags such as vision loss, vomiting, neck stiffness, or neurological deficits noted.

# Plan

- **Headache, probable migraine:**
  - Recommend optimization of NSAID therapy: patient may take ibuprofen 400 mg (two tablets) three times daily as tolerated.
  - Consider prescribing naproxen as alternative NSAID for pain management.
  - Consider addition of paracetamol (acetaminophen) or codeine as adjunct pain relief.
  - Educate patient to keep a headache diary to identify potential triggers.
  - Advise follow-up in 1–2 weeks to reassess symptoms and discuss prophylactic migraine treatment if headaches become frequent.
  - Urge patient to seek urgent care if symptoms worsen or new neurological symptoms develop (vision changes, weakness, speech difficulties).
  - Recommend repeat eye examination due to eye strain and prolonged computer exposure.
  - Encourage hydration and stress management as supportive measures.
  - Document smoking and social alcohol use; advise moderation.
