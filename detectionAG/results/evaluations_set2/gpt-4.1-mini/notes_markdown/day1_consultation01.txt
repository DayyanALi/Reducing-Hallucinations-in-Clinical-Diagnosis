## Assessment

1. **Acute Gastroenteritis**  
   Loose watery diarrhea, abdominal cramping, vomiting, and subjective fever following ingestion of potentially contaminated food likely consistent with viral or bacterial gastroenteritis. No blood in stool or vomit reduces concern for invasive bacterial infection but cannot exclude it.  
   Differential diagnosis: viral gastroenteritis, bacterial gastroenteritis (foodborne), other causes of acute diarrhea (less likely given history).

2. **Asthma, well controlled**  
   No exacerbation reported during this episode.

---

## Plan

**Acute Gastroenteritis:**  
- Conservative management with hydration and rest  
- Encourage oral fluids including electrolyte solutions (e.g., Dioralyte) to prevent dehydration  
- Symptomatic treatment with paracetamol (acetaminophen), 2 tablets up to 4 times daily as needed for fever and malaise  
- Advise taking 2-3 days off work to allow recovery and reduce transmission risk  
- Monitoring: Observe for worsening symptoms, persistent diarrhea beyond 3-4 days, blood in stool, or new fever  
- Follow-up if symptoms fail to improve or worsen within 3-4 days for possible stool studies and further evaluation

**Asthma:**  
- Continue current inhaler regimen as previously prescribed

**Patient Education:**  
- Explanation of gastroenteritis and expected course  
- Importance of hydration and rest  
- When to seek urgent care (severe dehydration, blood in stool, inability to keep fluids down)

**Disposition:**  
- Outpatient management with instructions for follow-up if no improvement
