## Assessment

1. **Acute infectious gastroenteritis:**  
Clinical features of viral gastroenteritis with watery diarrhea, vomiting, abdominal pain, and fever. Exposure likely from brother with similar symptoms. Needs stool testing to exclude bacterial causes due to severity and persistence.

2. **Risk of dehydration:**  
Inability to retain fluids with ongoing vomiting raises risk of dehydration. Passing urine an hour ago is reassuring but hydration status should be clinically assessed.

---

## Plan

- Urgent clinic appointment for clinical assessment of hydration and collection of stool sample for microbiological testing.  
- Advise continuation of oral rehydration salts in small frequent sips to minimize vomiting.  
- Recommend small, bland diet (toast, biscuits); avoid fruit, vegetables, milk.  
- Monitor for worsening symptoms, especially inability to keep fluids, decreased urine output, dizziness, or increasing abdominal pain.  
- If worsening, patient advised to attend Accident & Emergency urgently.  
- Provide patient education on supportive care and signs of dehydration.  
- Follow-up through clinic visit and symptomatic management.
