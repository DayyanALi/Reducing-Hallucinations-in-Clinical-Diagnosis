## Assessment

1. **Acute severe headache with photophobia and visual disturbance**
   - Sudden onset, persistent, “blinding” headache for three days; all over but worse over right eye; not fully relieved by Ibuprofen. Associated with photophobia and “shooting lights,” but no neurologic deficits, nausea, or vomiting. Visual disturbance is atypical. Family history of migraine. Red flag symptoms (onset, severity, visual changes), but no fever, focal neuro symptoms, or meningism reported.
   - Differential: Migraine (with aura vs. without), less likely cluster headache, tension-type headache, secondary headache (e.g., intracranial pathology such as subarachnoid hemorrhage or mass—no evidence but needs in-person neuro exam), medication overuse headache, hormonal (OC pill association possible).
