Subjective:
• 27-year-old male, John Doe, recent wasp/bee sting on right arm (few minutes ago)
• Complains of mild breathing difficulty, lip swelling sensation, tongue feels weird
• Rash and slight redness with puncture mark on arm; no significant swelling noted
• No acute distress or severe breathing difficulty at time of consult
• PMH: Eczema
• Allergic to penicillin
• No history of anaphylaxis or EpiPen use
• Occasional anxiety, no meds for it
• Works as computer scientist, lives alone with a cat
• No known pet or food allergies
• Denies fever or other systemic symptoms

Objective:
• Lips not visibly swollen
• Mild rash and redness at sting site without swelling or signs of infection
• Breathing normal, no stridor or respiratory distress observed
• Oral cavity and throat exam unremarkable, no airway swelling

Assessment:
1. Localized allergic reaction to insect sting; mild rash and discomfort without systemic involvement
2. Anxiety contributing to perceived breathing difficulty

Plan:
• Advise OTC oral antihistamines (Piriton or Chlorphenamine) 4 mg up to 6x/day or once daily as preferred
• Monitor for signs of anaphylaxis: lip swelling, worsening breathing difficulty, stridor
• Seek urgent care if symptoms worsen or rapid progression occurs
• Educate patient on anaphylaxis symptoms; provide informational link
• No allergy testing or antibiotics indicated currently; consider if recurrent reactions or signs of infection develop
• Reassure and encourage follow-up if concerns persist or new symptoms arise
