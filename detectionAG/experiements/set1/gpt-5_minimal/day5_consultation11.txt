A - Assessment:
1) R knee overuse injury—likely patellofemoral pain syndrome vs meniscal irritation; no red flags (no locking/giving way, afebrile, resolved transient swelling); provoked by running and flex→extend, clicking reported.
2) Episodic R toe paresthesia post-run—transient; likely exertional/positional; no persistent neurovascular signs.

P - Plan:
• Activity modification: Temporarily reduce/hold running; cross-train low impact; avoid painful activities and gym movements provoking clicking.
• RICE: Continue ice after activity; consider short course compression and relative rest.
• Meds: Start prescribed stronger NSAID regularly with food; avoid concurrent ibuprofen/naproxen; review PRN use of melatonin/Xanax unchanged.
• Referral: Physiotherapy for comprehensive knee assessment, gait/strengthening (quad/hip), biomechanics; consider imaging if persistent sx or exam suggests internal derangement.
• Safety-net: Urgent review if persistent/worsening swelling, heat, redness, fever, inability to flex/extend, mechanical locking, instability/giving way, or neurovascular changes.
• Follow-up: If physio wait long, schedule in-clinic exam; reassess response to NSAID and activity modification.
