## Assessment

**Problems:**  
1. Left ear hearing loss with associated tinnitus and dizziness for 6 weeks.  
   - Likely causes include cerumen impaction causing conductive hearing loss or inner ear pathology.  
   - Differential diagnosis includes earwax impaction, otitis media or externa, cochlear or vestibular pathology, and possible early vestibular dysfunction.  
   - Tinnitus etiology uncertain; acknowledged as a common, poorly understood symptom potentially linked to hearing loss.  
2. Dizziness, mild, non-vertiginous, no cardiovascular symptoms noted.  

---

## Plan

**1. Left ear hearing loss and tinnitus:**  
- Schedule face-to-face otoscopic examination by a general practitioner within 4–5 days to rule out common causes such as wax impaction or eardrum pathology.  
- Provide patient education about tinnitus, including a link to a recommended leaflet regarding tinnitus understanding and management.  
- If ear examination reveals wax impaction, removal will be attempted to relieve symptoms.  
- If no physical blockage is found, further assessment and management will be considered based on clinical findings.  

**2. Dizziness:**  
- Currently mild and non-urgent; monitor symptoms.  
- No immediate cardiac or neurological symptoms, so no further acute workup indicated at this time.  

**3. Follow-up:**  
- Patient advised to contact support team to schedule the clinic appointment.  
- If symptoms worsen or new concerning symptoms develop (e.g., severe dizziness, vertigo, ear pain, discharge, fever), patient should seek prompt medical care or book a new appointment.
