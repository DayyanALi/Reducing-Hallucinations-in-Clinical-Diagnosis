## Assessment

1. **Lower abdominal pain, 2-day duration, worsening, with pink urine and mild nausea**  
   Differential diagnosis includes urinary tract infection (possible cystitis or pyelonephritis), gastrointestinal causes (e.g., gastritis, early appendicitis unlikely without migration), pregnancy-related causes (possible ectopic if pregnant), or other genitourinary pathology. The presence of pink urine raises concern for hematuria or urinary infection.

2. **Possible urinary tract infection (UTI)**  
   Symptoms and pink urine suggest UTI; however, absence of dysuria is atypical. Increased frequency is present but may be related to fluid intake.

3. **Pregnancy status unknown; needs confirmation**  
   Patient uses Implanon but acknowledges theoretical failure. Pregnancy test required to rule out pregnancy-related causes of abdominal pain.

---

## Plan

**Diagnostics:**  
- Arrange urine analysis and culture  
- Order urine pregnancy test

**Therapeutics:**  
- Empiric antibiotic therapy started with Trimethoprim 160 mg orally twice daily for three days for presumed UTI  
- Recommend paracetamol (acetaminophen) for pain relief; dose not documented  
- Encourage adequate hydration  
- Suggest cranberry juice as adjunct to help urinary symptoms  

**Monitoring:**  
- Monitor for worsening abdominal pain, persistent or worsening fever, vomiting  
- Follow-up if symptoms worsen or do not improve within few days  

**Consultations:**  
- None requested at this time

**Patient Instructions:**  
- Take prescribed antibiotics as directed  
- Perform pregnancy test to exclude pregnancy  
- Return or contact if symptoms worsen or new symptoms develop (e.g., high fever, increased pain, vomiting)
