## A - Assessment

1. **Left elbow swelling, likely bursitis**  
   Presentation suggests inflammation of the bursa around the left elbow with swelling and mild warmth but no pain or systemic signs of infection. Septic arthritis unlikely at this stage given lack of severe pain, redness, or systemic symptoms.

2. **Osteoarthritis (history)**  
   Patient has a known history of osteoarthritis, may contribute to joint symptoms but not acute swelling.

3. **Peanut allergy**  
   Known allergy; no acute reaction noted.

## P - Plan

**Left elbow swelling (suspected bursitis):**  
- Start non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID): Ibuprofen 400 mg orally twice daily after food; advise to discontinue if heartburn or intolerance develops.  
- Arrange blood tests to assess inflammatory markers, infection, rheumatologic antibodies, and screen for gout.  
- Send blood test requisition forms to patient's residence with instructions for where to have blood drawn.  
- Schedule follow-up appointment (telephonic or video preferred, face-to-face if worsening symptoms occur) after blood test results are available (anticipated 5-6 days after test).  
- Educate patient about warning signs requiring urgent evaluation: increased redness, severe pain, spreading erythema, or systemic illness that may indicate infection requiring prompt assessment.

**Osteoarthritis:**  
- Continue current management (no medications documented).

## Patient Instructions

- Take ibuprofen 400 mg twice daily with food for elbow swelling/inflammation.  
- Monitor elbow for changes in redness, pain, or swelling. Seek immediate care if symptoms worsen or signs of infection develop.  
- Complete blood tests when forms arrive and follow-up as scheduled.  
- Report any side effects from ibuprofen, such as heartburn, and stop medication if they occur.
