## A – Assessment

1. **Dysuria, mild lower abdominal pain, and one episode of hematuria**  
   - Likely uncomplicated urinary tract infection (UTI) based on recent onset dysuria, crampy lower abdominal pain, malodorous urine, and mild hematuria, in sexually active young woman without red flag features.  
   - No urinary frequency or urgency. No fever, back/flank pain, or systemic symptoms reported.  
   - No vaginal discharge, making urethritis/cervicitis less likely, but sexually transmitted infection should be considered given sexual history and lack of prior STD testing.
   - Low suspicion for pyelonephritis given absence of constitutional and back symptoms.

   **Differential:**
   - Uncomplicated UTI (most likely)
   - Sexually transmitted infection (eg, Chlamydia, Gonorrhea)
   - Urethritis of non-infectious etiology
   - Interstitial cystitis (less likely, acute presentation)
   - Vaginitis (less likely – denies discharge)
