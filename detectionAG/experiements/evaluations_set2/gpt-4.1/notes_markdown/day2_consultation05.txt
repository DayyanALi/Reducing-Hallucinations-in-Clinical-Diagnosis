## S - Subjective

**Chief Complaint:**  
Episodes of weakness, numbness, and "weird sensations" in right hand and arm, occurring over past 1.5–2 months.

**History of Present Illness:**  
John Smith, a 32-year-old male, reports experiencing unusual symptoms over the past 1.5–2 months. Initial symptom was pronounced tiredness lasting a few days, followed by transient improvement. He subsequently developed episodes of numbness and "pins and needles" sensation in his right hand, accompanied by perceived weakness in the right arm. During episodes, he sometimes struggles to hold a cup and experiences mild difficulty drinking; he also notes an abnormal sensation in the throat but no frank swallowing or speech difficulty. Occasionally, his leg feels weaker and colleagues have commented on a slightly abnormal gait during these incidents, lasting 3-4 days at a time. These episodes, which initially occurred once per week to once per month, have become more frequent—now occurring a couple of times daily. No persistent symptoms reported between episodes.  

No visual changes other than baseline (wears glasses). No difficulties with speech. No chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, or other systemic symptoms reported. No persistent or current symptoms at time of consult.

## A - Assessment

1. **Recurrent, transient right-sided numbness, weakness, and abnormal gait**
   - Episodic neurological symptoms (right hand/arm/leg) with complete resolution between episodes, increased frequency, associated with abnormal gait and subtle swallowing difficulty during attacks.  
   - Differential: Transient ischemic attacks (TIAs/mini-strokes) [presumptive diagnosis], focal seizures, demyelinating process (e.g., multiple sclerosis), migraine with aura, functional neurologic disorder, metabolic/toxic causes, less likely structural lesion. Family history of stroke supports vascular risk consideration.  
2. **Self-initiated aspirin therapy**
   - Patient commenced aspirin without medical advice; risk/benefit of primary prevention unclear in this context, especially at young age and with normal prior cardiovascular risk profile.
