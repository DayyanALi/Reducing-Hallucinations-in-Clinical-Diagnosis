## Assessment

1. **New-onset dry, itchy, red skin on arms and hands likely irritant or allergic dermatitis**
   - Symptoms began after recent swimming. Distribution mostly arms/hands. No prior episodes. No evidence of infection (no fever, no discharge), and no systemic symptoms beyond tiredness. Denies spots, bleeding, or cracked skin. Potential contributory factors include work-related stress and possible irritant/allergic exposure during swimming.
   - Differential includes atopic dermatitis, irritant contact dermatitis (possibly from pool chemicals), allergic contact dermatitis, dry skin (xerosis), or less likely, early presentation of another dermatologic disorder. No features suggesting infection or systemic illness.

---

## Plan

1. **Suspected dermatitis**
   - Educate patient with informational leaflet about dermatitis.
   - Start topical emollients: apply to affected area multiple times daily ("paste on... throughout the day for the next few days").
   - Consider topical corticosteroid (steroid cream), prescription provided or offered (specific name/dose not documented).
   - Consider oral/per os (PO) antihistamines for itchiness (e.g., Piriton [chlorpheniramine] or loratadine; patient may obtain over-the-counter).
   - Paracetamol and other painkillers not indicated at present.
   - Advise patient to monitor for improvement over the next few days.
   - If no improvement or worsening of symptoms, advised to return for reassessment in one week.
