## A - Assessment

1. **Asthma exacerbation (mild/moderate)**
   - Recent onset wheezing, shortness of breath, cough, and subjective fever in a patient with known asthma. Likely triggers: viral illness and/or possible allergen exposure (history of hay fever, timing with possible pollen season). No evidence of severe exacerbation (no nocturnal symptoms, able to speak in full sentences most of the time, no chest pain, no resting dyspnea, no hemoptysis).
   - Differential: viral upper respiratory infection, asthma triggered by allergens, less likely cardiac etiology, pneumonia, or other pulmonary pathology.

2. **Allergic rhinitis/hay fever**
   - History of hay fever; possibly contributing to asthma flare.

## P - Plan

### Asthma Exacerbation
- Prescribe a peak flow meter for home monitoring.
- Prescribe a spacer to optimize inhaler delivery.
- Advise patient to use salbutamol inhaler (2–4 puffs) every 4 hours as needed.
- Advise to seek immediate assistance if symptoms worsen, fever returns, or ability to complete sentences declines.
- Arrange follow-up review in approximately one week or sooner if symptoms deteriorate.

### Allergic Rhinitis
- Recommend starting a regular antihistamine, to be obtained from pharmacy; pharmacist to advise on selection and use.

### Monitoring
- Patient to document peak flow measurements morning and evening for one week and review results at follow-up.
- Monitor symptom progression/worsening and medication usage.
